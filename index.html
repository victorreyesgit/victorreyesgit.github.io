Research

I am a PhD researcher in Computer Science at the University of Geneva.

My research focuses on the geometric and probabilistic foundations of high-dimensional data analysis. I am particularly interested in understanding how dimensionality, distance distributions, and neighborhood structures shape the behavior of data analysis methods.

My work explores the connections between:

  • Proximity and Data Geometry: Mathematical foundations of proximity, distance-based relationships, and neighborhood structures in data.
  • Intrinsic Dimensionality: Understanding the effective dimensionality of data and its implications for geometric and statistical methods.
  • Hubness: Investigating the emergence of hubs in high-dimensional spaces, their geometric and probabilistic properties, and their impact on nearest-neighbor methods.
  • Dimensionality Reduction: Studying dimensionality reduction methods, their geometric properties, and how they preserve or alter neighborhood relationships.
  • Probability and Nearest Neighbors: Developing probabilistic models of nearest-neighbor distributions, dependencies, and neighborhood assumptions.

My broader goal is to develop mathematical tools and theoretical frameworks that provide a deeper understanding of the geometry of data, connecting theoretical insights with practical algorithms, computational experiments, and interactive visualizations.
